## Direction contract

THESIS: The threshold to Century Road is a wire dispatch arriving, not a marketing hero — the visitor watches today's date print itself in like a telegraph bulletin before the masthead resolves, refusing the generic "big background image + headline + CTA" SaaS-landing arrangement.

OWN-WORLD: Bulletin ivory (#F6F1E4) / newsroom black (#141210) per system theme, newsprint ink / ivory white text, wire-red (#C81E3A light / #E54D61 dark) reserved for the CTA only. Masthead in Oswald 600, the ticking dateline in the existing monospace dateline treatment (0.6875rem, uppercase, tracking-wider) — the exact device already used on every event card's date stamp, not a new one. Zero radius, no shadow, no gradient, hairline rules only. Photography (added after initial ship, user-requested, then user-referenced against the well-known "infinite logo carousel" pattern) is admitted as a first-time material for this system, but on the system's terms: full color is refused (One Voice Rule reserves saturation for wire-red alone) — every photo runs through `grayscale` + `contrast` — frames are separated by hairline seams (the same material language as every rule elsewhere in this system, not a card border) — and a solid, flat scrim sits between photos and text (never a gradient or blur as decoration; the one gradient in this build is a CSS edge-fade *mask*, a technical device for the marquee pattern, not a visible color gradient). A loading tile shows `bg-muted`, not `bg-background` — a flat, in-system tone that reads as "a frame waiting to develop" rather than a hole matching the page behind it — and crossfades to the photo over 700ms once it arrives; not a shimmer/skeleton animation (this system doesn't have one and this isn't the place to invent one), just an opacity transition already covered by the reduced-motion kill-switch.

STORY: The visitor understands they've reached a live archive that updates daily, not a static site — the sequence itself proves "today" is real before any content does. Behind them, three filmstrips of real photography from a genuinely different, randomly-picked day of history scroll past continuously and seamlessly, alternating direction row to row, hinting at the archive's breadth before they've clicked anything. They watch, then press "Entra" into the Mappa.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Centered column on the fixed theme background, full viewport height, no sidebar or header chrome (a true threshold, outside the app shell). A monospace dateline (today's real date, locale-formatted) types in first, alone. Then "CENTURY ROAD" resolves above it in large Oswald caps, "press archive" beneath in the label voice. A hairline rule. Then the wire-red "Entra" button fades in last, sized to the 44px touch floor. Full-bleed behind all of it: three stacked horizontal bands (each 1/3 viewport height), each a seamless infinite filmstrip drawing a distinct slice of the same photo pool (real Wikimedia images tied to real events of one randomly-picked day, not today's — see `useHistoryPhotoCarousel`), each frame carrying a compact year plate, edges soft-masked (`mask-image` with a `-webkit-mask-image` twin for Safari, which never shipped the unprefixed property). Rows alternate direction (top: left-to-right, middle: right-to-left, bottom: left-to-right — each opposite its neighbor, user-requested) and run at 18-28s per loop (tuned down from an initial 45-70s cut once the user reported the motion read as static at a glance — the loop distance is a full viewport-plus, so a full cycle isn't what should be perceptible, the first second or two of drift is), staying different per row so the three never look mechanically synced. The photo pool draws 24 images per visit (8 unique per row, 16 once doubled), excluding SVGs (heraldry/flags/maps, not "real historical photographs" per the brief, and measurably the slowest Commons thumbnails to generate on a cold cache) — a real day's pool of qualifying raster entries runs 30-55+ even after that exclusion, measured directly against the backend, so 24 has real headroom. The seamless-loop technique: each row's track is its slice of the pool twice back to back, animated exactly ∓50% (direction per row via `animation-direction: reverse`), so the seam is invisible. Reduced motion (OS or the in-app override, read once per visit) removes the duplication and all three animations entirely: three static rows, no loop, no scroll. Below the fold, reachable by scroll: a quiet footer consolidating the app's existing data-source attributions (Wikipedia, Wikimedia Commons, OpenStreetMap/OpenFreeMap, Natural Earth, GitHub), reusing existing attribution copy; no per-photo credit UI exists with this many photos visible at once — attribution for images is a general Commons link, not per-frame.

FORM: Structural candidate 4 of 7 on the ordered grounded list for this surface (front-page splash, dateline countdown, dispatch stack, **wire ticker awakening**, archive vault threshold, two-column bulletin, quiet reading room) — dealt to the table at position 4 of the roll (dealt indices 7,6,4) and locked by the user. Seed key 3e1679c1. Six user-requested follow-ups since: a single-photo Ken Burns crossfade (superseded, removed), a single seamless filmstrip built against a named external reference ("Seamless Infinite Logo Carousel", freefrontend.com/css-carousels/), split into three alternating-direction rows, a perceptibility/load-speed pass (durations cut roughly in half), a density pass raising the photo count, then a reliability pass (SVG exclusion + muted-placeholder crossfade, see FIRST VIEWPORT/OWN-WORLD/FINISH) after the density pass alone didn't fully resolve gaps some individual slow-loading tiles were still causing — all extend the locked FIRST VIEWPORT rather than reopening FORM; the world (OWN-WORLD) held through every iteration.

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance. Load-speed pass: `<link rel="preconnect">` added in `index.html` for `upload.wikimedia.org`/`thumb.wikimedia.org`, `decoding="async"` throughout. `loading="lazy"` was tried on the duplicated loop-half and reverted (caused the same class of gap this whole thread kept chasing: this strip never stops scrolling, so a deferred fetch can still be untriggered when the loop needs it) — replaced with `fetchPriority` (`low` on the duplicate half, `auto` on the first). Root-caused the *remaining* gaps (present even after raising the photo count to 24) to individual slow Commons thumbnails — some measured 20s+ on a cold cache, well past a single loop — not pool scarcity (a real day's raster-only pool measured 30-55+, always comfortably above 24). Two fixes: exclude SVG sources (the slowest class, and off-brief anyway — see OWN-WORLD), and stop the remaining slow tiles from reading as broken (muted placeholder + crossfade, see OWN-WORLD) since eliminating third-party image latency entirely isn't possible from this frontend. Known open item, disclosed rather than silently resolved: the filmstrip's photo pool is genuinely unfiltered real history (any real Wikipedia "selected"/"events" entry with a qualifying raster image, from a random day) — it can surface intense subject matter (war, atrocity, named perpetrators) on the very first screen a new visitor sees, across three rows of simultaneously-visible photos. No content filter exists yet; whether one should is a product/editorial decision, not a design one, and is unresolved.
